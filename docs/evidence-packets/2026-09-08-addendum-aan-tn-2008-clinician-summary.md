## Verbatim grade statements carried into `gronseth-aan-efns-tn-2008.quoted_text`

All transcribed from the clinician summary's Pharmacological Treatment and Diagnostic sections:

- "Strong evidence supports that carbamazepine should be offered to treat CTN pain (Level A)."
- "Good evidence supports that oxcarbazepine should be considered to treat CTN pain (Level B)."
- "The two drugs to consider as first-line therapy in TN are CBZ (200-1200 mg/day) and OXC (600-1800 mg/day). Although the evidence for CBZ is stronger than for OXC, the latter may pose fewer safety concerns."
- "Weak evidence supports that baclofen, lamotrigine, and pimozide may be considered to treat CTN pain (Level C)."
- "There is little evidence to guide the clinician on the treatment of TN patients who fail first-line therapy. Some evidence supports add-on therapy with lamotrigine or a switch to baclofen (pimozide being no longer in use)."
- "There is insufficient evidence to support or refute the effectiveness of any medication in treating pain in STN (Level U)."
- "There is insufficient evidence to support or refute the efficacy of intravenous medications for the treatment of pain from TN (Level U)."
- Surgical, diagnostic, and AAN grade-definition statements as carried in the record's quoted_text.

## Scope notes (never-drift relevant)

- Every drug grade is scoped to **CTN (classic trigeminal neuralgia)**; secondary TN (STN) is graded **Level U** separately. Rendered surfaces must carry the CTN scope (pre-gate condition C2).
- The summary itself instructs: "refer to the full guideline ... at www.aan.com". A newer society guideline exists and is not held (Bendtsen et al., EAN 2019, doi 10.1111/ene.13950); acquisition tracked in TASKS.md; grade-bearing rows carry an "(AAN 2008)" vintage label until it is read.
