| B1 | Is the rule safe for a control that feeds the window badge? | **Pass.** Code check: `leadingZeroHour` needs the hour text to be exactly two characters starting with "0". It sets h12 = hh (1-9), periodIdx 0 and isMilitary true, which gives hourIdx = hh - 1. The direction analysis is what makes this safe. The old trap (0800 kept PM) could move LKW 12 h *later*, making the patient look falsely in-window, the dangerous direction for thrombolysis. The new trap (0630 meant as PM, read as AM) can only move LKW *earlier*, making the patient look falsely out of window: an error of omission, never falsely qualifying. The same holds for the wake field: an earlier wake time means more elapsed time since symptoms were noticed, and a wake time at or before bedtime is rejected. The non-digits are stripped, the leading zero then forces AM, and before this change these inputs kept the selected period. This is also in the conservative direction. Follow-up. |

## Rationale
Change A applies a publisher correction to a live bedside drug step. It changes exactly one route token, keeps dose, concentration, volume, trigger and alternative unchanged, and moves the card to the conventional and now corrected IM route. Provenance rests on one reader reading twice. That is accepted here because the "before" text cross-checks word for word against the held first proof, the bibliographic details are consistent across two dated artifacts, and the alternative is to keep showing a route the publisher has withdrawn. A second human reading and re-verification against the final PDF are required follow-ups. Change B removes a real way for a typed morning time to become a PM time, which could make a patient look falsely in-window for thrombolysis. Any error the new rule introduces can only make LKW earlier, an error of omission, and the Sleep tab reads the result back in AM/PM. The remaining gaps (no read-back in the Specific Time tab, unrecognized "p" / "p.m." suffixes, period inheritance, a few boundary tests) are improvements and do not meet any mandatory-block condition. I checked all eight: no drift in the changed text, no synthesis, no registered claim with an unresolvable source, no quoted_text involved, this artifact meets #5, no last_reviewed refresh, no trial or statistics display, and no new trial entry.
